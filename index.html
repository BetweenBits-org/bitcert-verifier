Recipient

Whose record this is

Attribution is the issuer's claim, bound into the signature. It becomes more than a claim when the recipient's identifier is supplied (type 1) or the registered key holder signs a challenge (type 2).

Trust roots

What the aux commitment binds

The 32-byte aux_commitment in the anchor payload commits the issuer trust list, the revocation list and the envelope root at anchor time. Key validity is judged at the block time, never at the self-reported issue time.

Presenter

Is the person presenting this the registered recipient?

This record is bound to a passkey public key. Generate a one-time challenge here, offline, let the recipient sign it on the console origin, and check the result. A missing or failed presentation degrades the verdict to "presenter: not available" - it never rejects.

Optional

On-chain confirmation

The only Bitcoin-dependent step. Look up on a source of your choosing - never BitCert. Offline? Skip it; the checks above already bind the record to that exact transaction id.

Uses the explorer's public REST API (/api/tx/<txid>). For regtest/private anchors use your self-hosted explorer. For mainnet/testnet, any public explorer works.
What this proves. (1) the record's leaf is included in the Merkle root; (2) that root is the value committed inside the transaction's anchor output; (3) those anchor output bytes belong to exactly that anchor txid (double-SHA-256 of the raw transaction, computed here). The on-chain confirmation step then shows that txid sits in a Bitcoin block. For an identity-bound issuance (bundle v4) the leaf is additionally an issuer's ES256 signature over the record, that key is proven to be in the trust list the same anchor commits, the record is proven not revoked at anchor time, and - when the recipient registered a passkey - the presenter can prove they hold it. Verdicts have four grades (valid · warning · undetermined · rejected); a missing proof lowers the grade rather than being assumed.
